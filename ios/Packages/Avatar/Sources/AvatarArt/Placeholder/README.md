# Placeholder character

Stand-in art and motion until the character design is decided. The formats are the real thing.

- `rig.json`: the parts, back to front, each with its parent, pivot and art layers. Every part is drawn on the shared 240×320 canvas in its rest pose, so artists export each layer from one artboard. `defaults` names the variant a slot wears when a look leaves it out: round eyes and a smile, the face every animal keeps.
- `art/base/<art>.svg`: parts every character shares (the shadow). `art/<slot>/<variant>/<art>.svg`: what a look picks per slot: `body` (`peach` for people, whose skin tone is a swatch; species and coat for animals: cats, dogs, bears, rabbits), `hair`, the face (`eyes`, `brows`, `mouth`, `cheeks`, `glasses`), `hat`, `top`, `bottom`, `prop`. Each mouth draws `closed` and `open`, which the cheers swap between. People pick hair and a face (`EditorChoices.slots(for:)`; `editor.json` says which species are people); animals' faces come with their coat. A variant may skip a part (people and bears draw no `tail`). Adding a variant is adding its folder: `PlaceholderCharacter.variants(for:)` lists what's bundled, and the tests run every variant through every persona's clips. Nothing may leave the canvas mid-jump, which is why rabbit ears droop to the sides instead of standing up. Animals wear hats but no hair.
- `art/<slot>/<variant>/tones.json`: the colors a look may recolor in that variant, `{"version": 1, "base": "#2D5BA0", "shades": ["#28467E"]}`. `base` is the main color; `shades` are the outlines, folds and stripes drawn from it, which keep their relation to it (a darker outline stays as much darker). Every other color in the art (shoes, blush, highlights, trim) keeps its own. Hair, eyes, brows, top and bottom variants have one, and so do bodies with a plain coat; a patterned coat (a calico, a husky) keeps its colors and has none, as do hats and props. `PlaceholderCharacter.swatches(for:wearing:)` lists the preset colors the editor offers: all from the design system's palette: its scene and meaning colors for clothes, its character tones for skin, fur, hair and eyes. Brows offer the hair colors and follow the hair (the editor's `CharacterDraft`) until given their own.
- `editor.json`: what the character editor shows beyond every bundled variant: bodies grouped by species in display order (`person` shows only `peach` and takes skin tones from the swatches; `"hair": true` marks the species that wears hair) and the props of this release. A bundled prop the list leaves out stays hidden.
- `clips/<persona>/idle.json` and `cheer.json`: each persona's idle loop and check-in cheer, as keyframe tracks per part and channel (`rotation` in degrees clockwise, `x`, `y`, `scaleX`, `scaleY`, `opacity`). A key's `ease` shapes the move into it: `linear`, `hold`, `easeIn`, `easeOut`, `easeInOut`, `backOut` or four cubic-Bézier numbers.
- Personas: `deadpan`, `energetic`, `showoff`, `drama`, `shy`. Every look plays every persona. Adding one is a new `clips/<name>/` folder with both clips, plus a `Persona` case of that name; the tests run over every case and check it binds, loops seamlessly, lands where its idle starts and stays on the canvas.
- SVG: flat paths, circles, ellipses and rects with hex fills and strokes. No transforms, gradients, filters, text or arcs (outline them).
